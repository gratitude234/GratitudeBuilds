## The need

Students do not need another pile of disconnected resources. They need a clear place to prepare, practise, and understand where they stand before an exam. JabuStudy was built around that practical need.

<DecisionCallout label="Product principle">
  Make the next useful study action obvious. Every tool should reduce uncertainty, not add another layer of admin.
</DecisionCallout>

## From scattered tasks to one study rhythm

I shaped the product around the moments students repeat most: finding useful material, testing recall, practising in a CBT-like flow, and checking academic progress. The experience keeps those tools close without making the interface feel heavy.

<ProductVisual variant="study" expanded caption="A visual study of the JabuStudy practice experience." />

## Design direction

The interface prioritises quick orientation, readable questions, clear progress, and low-friction movement between tools. On small screens, the most important action stays within reach and secondary controls stay quiet.

<FlowDiagram items="Choose a tool|Start focused practice|See progress|Return with context" />

## Building the complete system

I owned the product from the initial problem and interaction design through full-stack implementation, deployment, and iteration. That meant treating content structure, application state, performance, and the student-facing experience as one connected system.

## What changed

More than 1,500 students have used JabuStudy. The strongest signal has been direct student appreciation for how the product supports their study routine and CBT exam preparation.

<MetricGrid items="1,500+::Students reached|01::Founder and builder|Live::Still improving" />

## Next iteration

The work now is to deepen the feedback loop: make practice more adaptive, surface useful progress signals, and keep improving the experience around how students actually prepare.
